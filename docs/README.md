# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

OctoAcme manages cross-functional work through an iterative lifecycle with clear ownership, customer-focused outcomes, and regular communication. Projects begin with initiation to validate the business need, align stakeholders, define measurable success criteria, and confirm the initial timeline and resources. Approved initiatives move into planning, where the team creates a prioritized backlog, acceptance criteria, estimates, milestones, a Definition of Done, and a shared view of risks and dependencies.

During execution, work is tracked on a project board across Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done. The Project Manager coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, and communications; the Product Manager owns outcomes and prioritizes the backlog; Developers implement, test, review, and document changes; and QA/test roles validate quality and feature acceptance. The team collaborates through small pull requests, daily standups, weekly delivery and PM–Product syncs, milestone demos, and regular stakeholder updates.

Quality is built into delivery through unit and integration testing, CI-enforced linting and automated tests, security scanning, and end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows. Pull requests include issue links and acceptance criteria, require review approval, and merge only after the team’s quality checks pass. Progress and success are monitored through velocity, burndown, dashboards, and product metrics, while blockers follow a clear escalation path from team triage to the PM, Product Lead or dependent teams, sponsor, or the security incident process.

Release preparation requires merged acceptance criteria, passing CI and security checks, release notes, rollback planning, and smoke-test readiness. Deployments are verified in staging and production, then announced to stakeholders and support. After sprints, releases, incidents, or milestones, retrospectives capture lessons and convert the highest-priority improvements into owned, dated action items that feed back into the backlog and living process documentation.
